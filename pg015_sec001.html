(e)

Coordination of movements.

Ensure that the pace of your acting movements matches the scene’s context.

For example, if you act as if you are climbing a steep mountain, do not walk at a normal pace as if you are on flat ground.

(f)

Audience engagement.

Maintain eye contact with the audience to keep them involved in the performance.

Body postures and positioning in facing the audience are the main tools that will help you ensure your audience is watching and following the performance.

Develop the skill to communicate with your audience using your eyes.

(g)

Organisation and flow of the performance.

Ensure the performance has a smooth and logical flow from beginning to end.

The sequence should be easy to follow, even without sound or words.

(h)

Regular exercise and flexibility.

Mime requires physical strength and flexibility.

Regular stage exercises can help improve body movements and performance.

(i)

Use of music and sound effects.

In mime, music and sound effects can be used to enhance emotions and guide the story.

They help the audience understand the scene more effectively.

Importance of mime

This art form plays a significant role in society and the world of performing arts.

Some of its benefits include:

(a)

Promoting the traditions and customs of the nation both locally and internationally;

(b)

Helping actors and others understand how to use their bodies to convey messages effectively;

(c)

Fostering creativity as it requires significant creativity to express emotions and events through body movements and gestures;

(d)

Serving as a teaching tool, especially for children and people with hearing impairments;

(e)

Entertaining as it is a unique and captivating form of performing art;

(f)

Touching deeply the emotions of the audience because it compels people to understand the message by observing gestures and movements only; and

(g)

Developing acting talent in mime,whereas actors learn to channel their emotions without relying on words.

This makes them better performers in other forms of acting.
